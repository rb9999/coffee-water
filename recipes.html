Roast recipe guide

Starting targets for magnesium, calcium, and alkalinity by roast level. Plug these into the calculator, brew, and adjust from taste.

Light roast

Preserve brightness and acidity; lean extraction to avoid sourness from under-extraction.

Magnesium

35–40 mg/L

Calcium

15 mg/L

Alkalinity

30–35 mg/L

Higher magnesium pulls out delicate, fruity notes. Lower alkalinity leaves acidity intact rather than buffering it away — light roasts already risk tasting sour or thin if under-extracted, so water that extracts efficiently helps.

  • Too sour or thin → increase calcium slightly for more body
  • Still muted despite high magnesium → check grind/brew ratio before adjusting water further
  • Tastes flat or dull → alkalinity may be too high — dial back toward 30 mg/L

Medium roast

Balanced extraction and body — the standard starting point.

Magnesium

30 mg/L

Calcium

20 mg/L

Alkalinity

50 mg/L

Moderate magnesium keeps sweetness and complexity present. Moderate calcium adds body without flattening the cup. Alkalinity buffers just enough acidity to round out the roast without muting it.

  • Too bright/sour → bump alkalinity to 55–60 mg/L
  • Tastes muted or flat → back off alkalinity to 40–45 mg/L, bump magnesium to 32–35 mg/L
  • Thin body → increase calcium to 22–25 mg/L

Dark roast

Tame bitterness and ashy notes; round out body to compensate for flavor complexity lost in roasting.

Magnesium

20–25 mg/L

Calcium

25–30 mg/L

Alkalinity

65–75 mg/L

Higher alkalinity buffers the acids that read as harsh or ashy in dark roasts. More calcium adds body/mouthfeel. Lower magnesium is used since aggressive extraction can pull out excess bitterness from dark-roasted beans.

  • Still bitter or ashy → push alkalinity toward 75–80 mg/L
  • Tastes flat/dead → alkalinity may be too high — pull back toward 60 mg/L
  • Watery mouthfeel → increase calcium to 30–35 mg/L

General adjustment logic

As roast gets darker: magnesium goes down, alkalinity goes up. Calcium moves modestly upward for added body. Change one variable at a time between batches and brew the same bean/recipe you know well, so you can isolate what the water changed. Keep notes — your dialed-in numbers for a given bean become your new baseline next time you buy it.
