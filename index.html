Mg stock = magnesium sulfate stock. Ca stock = calcium chloride stock. Alk stock = your selected alkalinity source stock (baking soda or potassium bicarbonate).

Making the stock solutions

Do this once per chemical (3 total: magnesium sulfate, calcium chloride, and your alkalinity source — baking soda by default, or potassium bicarbonate if you select that instead).

  1. Tare your scale, weigh the powder, and record the exact figure (e.g. 10.02g — don't round to 10).
  2. Add distilled water and record the exact water weight added (e.g. 91.85g) — no need to hit a round total.
  3. Stir or shake until fully dissolved.
  4. Calculate your true concentration: solute mass ÷ (solute mass + water mass) × 1000 = mg per gram of solution. Example: 10.02 ÷ 101.87 × 1000 = 98.4 mg/g.
  5. Enter that number into the matching "Stock concentrations" field above instead of leaving it at the default 100.
  6. Label the bottle (chemical name, measured mg/g, date made).
  7. Repeat for the other two chemicals in separate containers.

This weight-native method avoids the volume/density approximation entirely — every number is a direct scale reading, so it's more accurate than assuming a clean 100mg/mL from a graduated container.

To dose: weigh out the calculator's gram amounts directly onto your brew water on the same scale.
